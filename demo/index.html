Protocol Documentation, Expiry Schedule & Fees

Reference specification for Optionality's expiry schedule, onchain pool fee structure, Black-Scholes spread pricing arithmetic, and confidential SGX copy-trading architecture.

Expiry & Settlement Schedule 12:00 AM UTC+0 Daily Default + Any-Time Custom Expiry

parameterspecificationhow it works onchain
Default Daily Expiry12:00 AM (00:00) UTC+0 Every standard daily series expires at midnight UTC+0 (00:00:00 UTC) and is selected by default across the Options chain and interactive Chart.
Testnet Any-Time Expiry≥ 1 minute from entry Traders on Testnet / Local Sandbox can specify any custom expiry timestamp at least 1 minute after trade entry for rapid settlement testing.
Mainnet Any-Time Expiry≥ 1 day (24h) from entry On Mainnet, custom expiries must be at least 24 hours from the moment of entry alongside the standard 12:00 AM UTC+0 daily series.
Oracle Settlement Committee2-of-4 Quorum (≤ 100 bps) Settlement fixes require at least 2 fresh signatures from Kraken, OKX, Pyth, and the venue median index within 100 bps tolerance.

Fee Schedule & What the Venue Charges read from the contract

lineratewhere it is charged
Entry margin- Inside the premium you pay to open. Nothing is billed afterwards.
Early-close haircut- Inside the price the pool pays you back. Holding to expiry pays no haircut.
Settlementnone Permissionless: anyone can post the print and settle a ticket, and pays gas only.
Deposit / withdrawalnone NEP-141 transfer with 1 yoctoNEAR attached; the network fee is the whole cost.

Rounding is not neutral and is deliberately one-way: the entry premium rounds up and the exit value rounds down, so both of the cases someone could argue about favour the pool by at most one unit of the token, and never in a way a share price can drift on.

The arithmetic

Mark

mark = put(high) − put(low), calls mirrored. Black-Scholes on the median of three venues with a put-skew smile σ(K) = σ_atm · (1 + 0.35 · ln(S/K)), r = 0. The engine, not this page, does the maths.

Entry premium

ceil(contracts · mark · (1 + margin) / scale) · what the ticket costs. The buyer pays it once and it is never itemised on the ticket.

Split of that premium

venue = floor(premium · margin ÷ (1 + margin)), the rest joins the pool's equity. So of a margin uplift, - of the premium is the venue's and the remainder is the vault's own money · which is why it counts against what the vault can afford to write.

Early close

floor(contracts · mark · (1 − haircut) / scale) · what the pool pays to take the ticket back.

Collateral and payoff

max payout = contracts · width. The pool keeps the premium it collected, so what it actually risks is the worst case minus that; a ticket is only written while its reserve still fits inside equity. At expiry the ticket pays clamp(high − S, 0, width) for a put and clamp(S − low, 0, width) for a call, per contract, always rounded down.

Where the margin has got to

Margin on tickets still open (a void returns it, so nobody may spend it)-
Margin earned, sweepable by the venue-
Consolation pot-
Vault equity behind the open tickets-
Committed against worst cases-

The venue's slice on an open ticket is collateral against a refund, not revenue: sweeping it early would leave a void with nothing to pay from. It becomes sweepable only once the ticket has settled, closed or voided · and a sweep never touches equity, so collecting fees cannot dilute a share.
